Water credits, explained simply.

A water credit is proof that a real, measured volume of water was saved, recycled or returned to nature, beyond what would have happened anyway. Here's how they work and how you can earn them.

The idea

Make saving water worth something

Water is underpriced almost everywhere, so projects that save or recycle it often struggle to pay for themselves. Water credits fix that by giving verified water benefits a value that others can buy or report.

The idea is the same as carbon credits: measure a baseline, carry out an intervention, measure again, verify independently, and issue credits for the difference. One credit usually represents a fixed volume of water, such as one cubic metre (1,000 litres). The exact unit depends on the programme.

In India, the Green Credit Rules, 2023 (MoEFCC) list water management among the activities that can earn green credits. Draft methodologies for water harvesting have been published for consultation, and voluntary water-credit registries and corporate water-stewardship programmes are growing alongside them.

What makes a credit credible

  1. MeasurableMetered volumes, not estimates on paper
  2. AdditionalWouldn't have happened without the project
  3. Baseline-referencedCompared against a documented starting point
  4. Independently verifiedChecked by an accredited third party
  5. UniqueCounted once, never double-claimed
  6. Permanent & monitoredBenefit sustained over the crediting period

Eligible activities

Ways to create a water benefit

Recycling & reuse

Treated sewage or effluent replacing freshwater in industry, construction, landscaping, flushing or cooling towers.

Rainwater harvesting

Rooftop and surface runoff captured for use, reducing withdrawals from groundwater or municipal supply.

Groundwater recharge

Recharge wells, check dams, farm ponds and percolation tanks that replenish aquifers.

Agricultural efficiency

Drip and sprinkler irrigation, soil-moisture scheduling and crop shifts that cut water per acre.

Loss reduction

Leak detection, pressure management and metering that reduce non-revenue water in city networks.

Ecosystem restoration

Lake rejuvenation, wetland and watershed restoration that increase water storage and quality.
